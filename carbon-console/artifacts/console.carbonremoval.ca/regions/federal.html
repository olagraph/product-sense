Carbon removal policy environment

  • The Government of Canada operates an industrial carbon pricing system known as the Output-Based Pricing System (OBPS). This system is also used as a 'backstop' and made mandatory in any province or territory that does not develop its own equivalent pricing system. It is currently set at $95/tonne and escalates $15/tonne per year until it reaches $170/tonne by 2030.

  • CO2 storage regulations are the jurisidiction of provincial and territorial governments, except as it relates to cross-provincial border issues or storage under the seabed. The government recently held a consultation on offshore carbon dioxide storage.

  • The Government of Canada is currently finalizing its Direct Air Capture protocol and has made its intentions known it will pursue BECCS in the future.

  • Offsets as compliance option under carbon pricing system
    Yes

  • The Government of Canada has made multiple programs available to carbon removal technologies, including a CCUS ITC and a grants-based program known as the Energy Innovation Program. The CCUS ITC provides a 60% tax refund on eligible materials, currently limited to DAC, while the EIP funding stream is closed.

  • The Government of Canada, through its Low-carbon Fuel Procurement Program, will deploy CAD$10M out of a total fund of $135M toward purchasing credits from carbon removal projects through 2030.

  • Government of Canada is committed to holding consultations on the role carbon removal could play in helping to meet Canada’s 2035 and 2040 climate goals and create new economic opportunities.
